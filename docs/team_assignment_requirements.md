## Project Topics

### Option 1 — Reimplement a Paper

- Take a conference paper from **2015 or later** and reimplement the algorithm in it.
- Allowed venues: **AAAI, IJCAI, ICML, NeurIPS, ICLR, CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, NAACL, ACL, EMNLP** — *nothing else*.
- Choose a paper **without public codebases** on the Internet. **Reusing public code is not allowed.**

### Option 2 — Improve an Existing Codebase

- Take a conference paper from **2019 or later** and develop/implement an improvement on top of its existing codebase.
- Reusing existing source code is **allowed**.

### Option 3 — Regularization Study *(lower novelty & difficulty than Options 1–2)*

- Take a dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10, Sentiment Analysis) and a popular network (e.g., ResNet-18, Transformer).
- Experiment with **4–5 different regularization techniques** and analyze the differences in results.
- Do **not** repeat the model–dataset combination of your individual project.

## Presentation Scoring Criteria

All technical presentations use the formula:

$$
\text{Score} = \text{Content Quality} \times \text{Audience Comprehension}
$$

- In AI6103, **content quality** is mostly reflected by the report.
- For the presentation, the grade focuses mainly on **comprehension**, e.g.:
  - What is the problem definition and the technical approach?
  - Why is it better than others?
  - Is the presentation logically or randomly assembled?
  - Is it enjoyable — or do I have sore eyes and want to fall asleep?

Your job is to use **language and graphics to communicate**:

- Everything you show should have a point — a **take-away message**.
- There is a time and place for everything. For example, is animation good?
  - **Yes**, if it is integral to the presentation and helps the audience understand your message.
  - **No**, if it is animation for its own sake, or if it distracts from the main message.

---

## Presentation: Things to Do

- Explain the **problem definition**, the **technical approach**, the **results**, and the **take-away message**.
- Identify what matters most for the main argument and **present only those parts**.
- Explain that you are awesome — **in a measured way**:
  - *"LSTM does not model sequential information and we fixed that"* — simply not true.
  - *"By this data augmentation we created infinite training data"* — true in math, false on a computer.
- **Back up claims with evidence.**

## Presentation: Things Not to Do

- **Don't** read from a script without caring whether anyone understands.
- **Don't** follow a presentation formula as dogma (*"My professors/friends told me a formula. I don't care why, how, or if it works. I just follow."*).
- **Don't** include graphics that communicate nothing, existing only for the sake of graphics.
- **Don't** use tiny diagrams nobody can read (*"I checked the diagram box! Yeah!"*).
- **Don't** let extremely thick accents prevent communication (you may use an AI voice).
